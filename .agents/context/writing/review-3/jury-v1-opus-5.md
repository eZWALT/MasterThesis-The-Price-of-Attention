# JOB 2 — Substantive methodological review

## CRITICAL

**C1. The advertised turn carries ~3 s of extra silent server wait, and this is never named as a confound — including inside the Dataset B pre-onset window.**
Location: §5.1.2: "*On the one advertised turn, retrieval runs before the first token (median 3.03 s), so that silent wait is about 6 s: noticeable, still below 10 s, and **the only ads-versus-no-ads difference in server wait**.*" §6.2.5 lists three "structural confounds the design cannot remove"; latency is not one of them. §8.7 does not mention it.
Why it bites: Dataset B is Dᴮᵢ = (post − pre)_ad − (post − pre)_a∅ (Eq. 6.2). For an advertised turn the 4 s pre-window sits at the tail of a reply that was preceded by a 3 s silent wait; under a∅ there was no such wait. The two pre-windows are therefore not the same behavioural state — one follows a stall, the other does not — and the subtraction that is supposed to remove the baseline instead differences two non-comparable baselines. A low-frequency shift (δ, θ) is exactly what a differing pre-state would produce, which is exactly what the explicit-early tilt is. Behaviourally, a 6 s wait on the one advertised turn of every advertised condition is a plausible independent cause of "felt commercial pressure" and lower credibility, and it is present in all four advertised cells and absent from a∅. §7.2.2 reports "*Nine logged interaction measures × the three planned contrasts and the format × timing interaction give 36 exploratory tests, none with raw p < .05*", which cannot be reconciled with §5.1.2's own measured 2.69 s vs 2.89 s reply difference plus 3.03 s retrieval unless the logged measures are averaged over all four turns and dilute the single-turn effect — and that is not stated.
Change: add latency to §6.2.5 and §8.7; report per-turn latency for the advertised turn versus the matched a∅ turn; report Dᴮ with pre-window mean power as a covariate, or at minimum report the pre-window absolute δ and θ levels by condition so a reader can see whether the pre-states match.

**C2. The "noticed" item asks about an object that exists in only one of the two formats it is used to compare.**
Location: Tab. C.2, item 20: "*I felt I noticed or clicked on sponsored buttons during the conversation.*" §7.2.2: "*Noticed is agreement (≥ 5 of 7) on the detection item of the notice pair: the reading that stays low when nothing was inserted.*"
Why it bites: the implicit mention is not a button. Figure 6.1a shows it as bold inline text plus a trailing hyperlink; there is no button and no "sponsored" label. So the 46 % / 37 % "noticed" rates for the implicit cells (Tab. E.2) are the rate at which participants endorsed a statement about an object that was never present, and the headline format contrast on notice (−1.15, dz = −0.55, Holm < .001) is partly definitional rather than perceptual. The Abstract's "*about half of participants did not identify the mention as sponsored*" and §8.1's "*An implicit mention therefore reaches a large share of users without being recognised as advertising*" both rest on this item. The item is also double-barrelled ("noticed **or clicked**") in a study with zero clicks.
Change: report the format contrast on a format-neutral detection item, or state explicitly that the implicit detection rate is measured on an item whose referent does not exist in that condition and is therefore a lower bound of unknown tightness.

**C3. The single Holm-surviving confirmatory EEG result is a boundary result whose sensitivity analysis was run and not reported, and whose window is asymmetric between the two arms it compares.**
Location: §7.4: "*Under condition aggregation the only Holm cell is early minus late on posterior α (−0.22 dB, **Holm p = .0496**; raw Wilcoxon p = .021).*" Tab. 7.9: "*126 | – | the 4 s family does not hold at other widths*."
Why it bites: three things compound. (i) Holm p = .0496 means raw p ≈ .0165 × 3; it survives by four ten-thousandths. (ii) 126 width/cleaning tests were run and no number from them is printed for this cell (D9). (iii) The 37-epoch neighbourhood is "nearest that condition's visual onset". For an early advertisement onset is after turn 2, so the 148 s straddles onset; for a late advertisement onset is after turn 4, at the end of the conversation, so the 37 nearest epochs are necessarily almost all *before* onset. The contrast therefore compares a window centred mid-conversation against a window drawn from the conversation's back half — which is a time-on-task contrast, and posterior α rising with time on task is the textbook expectation. §8.3 and §9.1 acknowledge depth confounding in general terms ("*A late advertisement sits on a shorter, more fallback-labelled turn*") but never state that the windows themselves are asymmetrically placed relative to onset. This cell carries RQ8, an Abstract sentence and a Conclusion paragraph.
Change: print the cell at all five widths and both cleaning branches; state the mean signed offset of the 37-epoch window from onset in the early and late cells; and add the asymmetric windowing to §8.7.

**C4. The observed ρ = .80 exceeds what the reported reliabilities of its two component scores can support, and the thesis prints both numbers without drawing the inference.**
Location: §8.5: "*the interval is [.48, .93], and with a single-item trust score and an onset-locked α split-half of **.56** (Table 7.9), the lower bound is the number to carry.*" Tab. 7.9: "*onset-locked posterior α across early and late halves **.56***."
Why it bites: with reliability .56 on the EEG score and a single-item trust difference (§8.7: "*Trust is a single item per condition, so its person-level difference is the least reliable score in the battery*"), the disattenuated correlation is .80/√(.56 × r_trust). Even at a generous r_trust = .70 that is 1.28 — outside the admissible range. The correct reading is not "strong coupling" but "ρ = .80 at n = 18 is above the ceiling the measurement supports, so it is inflated by sampling noise." Leave-one-out stability (.77–.86) does not help, because leave-one-out is insensitive to the noise that inflated it. §9.1 then escalates: "*the cost is not distributed evenly across users, and the users who respond more strongly at the moment of insertion are also those who show the greatest loss of trust*" — a heterogeneity-of-cost claim from one correlation, in the same chapter that reports 0 of 130 moderation tests, and in a design where "*Neither mean trust nor mean posterior α changes significantly*" so there is no group-level trust loss to be unevenly distributed.
Change: report the attenuation calculation, frame the result as a bounded hypothesis with the lower bound and the reliability ceiling both stated, and delete the "cost is not distributed evenly" sentence from Chapter 9.

**C5. Task is not orthogonal to condition by construction, the claim that it is appears three times, and the realised assignment is not in the PDF.**
Location: §6.2.4: "*Tasks were rotated in Latin-square order; the five conditions were shuffled independently; the two sequences were then paired so that, **across participants, task identity is not confounded with condition**. The realised plan is written into the session log.*" §6.3 repeats: "*the order randomisation and Latin-square task rotation that keep condition unconfounded with task*."
Why it bites: independently shuffling conditions and pairing them to a Latin-square task order randomises the task × condition mapping; it does not balance it. With N = 54 and 25 task × condition cells that is ≈ 2.2 participants per cell, and 54 is not a multiple of 5, so exact balance is arithmetically unavailable. Task therefore *is* mildly confounded with condition in the realised sample. Nothing in the behavioural family models it: §7.2 says the adjusted check is a plain random-intercept model whose "*point estimates coincide with D, so only its standard errors can differ*", and §8.2 confirms "*Task type is not modelled as a moderator either*." Task is modelled only for the trajectory family (Kruskal–Wallis in §7.5, GEE for δ₂⁽ᵃ⁾). Given that the five tasks differ substantially in product category and price point, and that §8.7 already concedes "*The relevance and the quality of the particular product each participant was served is mostly unknown*", a task main effect on credibility and trust is entirely plausible.
Change: print the realised task × condition cross-tabulation; rerun the behavioural planned contrasts with task as a fixed effect and report the concordance as one row of Table 7.9; soften "not confounded" to "randomised, not balanced".

**M4. App. D.2 explains why that is nearly automatic: "*These are compositional: the five proportions sum to one by construction, so they cannot move independently.*" The parenthesis therefore reads as four independent band changes when it is one absolute low-frequency rise plus its arithmetic shadow. §8.3 handles this correctly ("*they are one phenomenon rather than five separate findings*"); the Abstract and Conclusion do not. **M5. The "not created by ICA" rebuttal is logically weak and cites an appendix that contains no such result.** §8.3: "*Removing ocular components is the preprocessing choice that could have created the tilt. **Leaving blinks in dilutes it and widens its spread, which is the opposite of an ocular-only account (Appendix D.1)**.*" Appendix D.1's entire statement on the no-ICA branch is "*Rerunning without ICA, and at 2, 8, 16, and 32 s, does not change the 4 s confirmatory family*" — no number for the tilt, which is not in the confirmatory family. # Three strongest aspects

1. **The two closing tables are the most honest thing in the thesis.** Tables 7.8 and 7.9 enumerate every family and every check with n, test count, Holm-surviving count and a representative estimate — roughly 3,900 tests, including the 2,560-test exploratory map with "*105 raw hits where 128 are expected; none survives Holm*" and the 336-test joined map with a Freedman–Lane family p. Most theses of this kind report the eight results that worked. Publishing the denominator, and putting the split-half reliabilities in the same table, is a real methodological contribution and it is what makes the rest of this review possible.

2. **§8.4 on the trajectory null is a model failure report.** It identifies three independent reasons the instrument could not see an insertion — saturation versus floor across the two contexts ("*Saturation and floor are not a spectrum; they are two ways to miss the same insertion*"), target/background overlap ("*fgenre labels 98 general guidance and information and only four purchasable products*", with 25 of 108 conversations where alignment is impossible by construction), and turn depth as the dominant signal ("*What a genre label tracks on this corpus is therefore turn depth*") — and refuses to let the null speak either for or against Definitions 1–6. The permutation null is also the right construction, since permuting g⁽ᵃ⁾ preserves the advertised-genre marginal and so accounts for exactly the overlap the section worries about.

3. **The EEG preprocessing is specified tightly enough to be audited from the PDF alone**, and two design choices are defended on the right grounds. The condition-blind quality profile ("*The windows were placed without reference to the event log, so it cannot preferentially clean one experimental condition*") is the correct safeguard and the three interpolated channels are named with their evidence. k = 37 is justified as the shortest eligible window rather than by outcome ("*k = 37 is the shortest eligible window, so every advertisement condition contributes exactly 37 epochs and n = 18 is retained*"), and the 4 s width is argued from the spectral settings and the onset uncertainty *before* any contrast, with the two neighbouring widths shown to fail in opposite directions. Appendix D.2's warning that the five relative powers "*cannot move independently*" is the correct reason not to correct across all sixteen measures, and it is the reason §8.3 gets the compositional cluster right where the Abstract does not.

# Examiner verdict

What would make me challenge this thesis is not the results — the behavioural family is competently executed and its headline (+1.27 on perceived manipulation, with format and timing as additive main effects and trust essentially unmoved over a short session) is credible and appropriately modest. It is that the discipline the thesis imposes on itself dissolves at exactly the surfaces a reader sees first and last. Chapter 8 is careful, self-critical and correct about what its estimands can bear; the Abstract then promotes a post hoc trust cell without its label, asserts an absence at implicit onset that Chapter 8 twice refuses, and compresses a compositional low-frequency cluster into four band arrows; and Chapter 9 issues, in one sentence, the disclosure obligation and the placement patience that Chapter 8 explicitly declined four pages earlier. A committee that reads only the Abstract, Table 8.1 and the Conclusion will come away with a stronger and partly different thesis than the one Chapter 7 supports — and that is a defence-losing gap, because it is the candidate's own Chapter 8 that supplies the rebuttal. Underneath that, three substantive things need answers before I would be comfortable: the three-second retrieval wait is a documented, systematic, ads-only difference that is never treated as a confound anywhere, least of all in the Dataset B pre-window; the single Holm-surviving confirmatory EEG cell is a p = .0496 result on asymmetrically placed windows whose 126 sensitivity variants were run and not reported, with the candidate's own summary table saying the family "does not hold at other widths"; and ρ = .80 at n = 18 sits above the ceiling its own reported reliabilities (.56, single-item) permit, yet Chapter 9 escalates it into a claim that advertising's cost falls unevenly across users. None of this is fatal. All of it is a fortnight's work: fix the Conclusion, print four missing tables, and reconcile Appendix D.2 with Table 7.8. The science underneath is sound enough to survive the correction, which is why the inconsistency score is the one I would press hardest on.
