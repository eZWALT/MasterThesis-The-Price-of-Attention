1.2 Disagreements found, each with two quoted locations

D1 — "Attention shift" claimed as a delivered contribution, then explicitly disclaimed twice.

Location A (Introduction §1.4, Contributions, p. 4): "We also propose some advancements in the underlying theory defining a advertisement taxonomy, expand the concept of intent and some novel metrics like attention shift."
Location B (Theory §3.1, Definition 6, p. 16): "Finally, we reserve the term attention shift for an ad-associated genre shift sub-case that can be causally attributed to the advertisement... Establishing such causal attribution requires additional analysis and is outside the scope of the present thesis."
Location C (Discussion §8.4, p. 85): "An observed δₖ⁽ᵃ⁾ would still be an association; calling it an attention shift would require a causal argument this study reserved and did not make." Locations B and C agree with each other and with the Abstract/Conclusion (which never once uses the phrase "attention shift" as an achieved result). Location A is the odd one out: it lists "attention shift" as a metric the thesis delivers, when the thesis's own theory chapter and discussion both say the opposite.

D2 — Conclusion issues a placement/timing prescription that Discussion explicitly disclaims.

Location A (Discussion §8.6, "Felt pressure as the user-side cost," p. 89): "This is not a recommendation to serve advertisements late or implicitly. The design did not measure the advertiser's side at all..."
Location B (Conclusion §9.1, "The overall picture," p. 98): "What follows for a platform... is not a placement rule but an obligation to disclose, to be patient with monetisation rather than rush into the earliest turns simply because they command attention, and to measure..." Location B denies being a "placement rule" in the same breath as recommending against early placement ("rather than rush into the earliest turns"), which is a timing recommendation in substance if not in name. It sits in tension with Location A's blanket disclaimer, issued one chapter earlier over the same evidence.

D3 — A specific numeric result appears for the first time in Discussion, not in Results.

Location A (Results, Fig. 7.8 heatmap and its prose, p. 71, and Table 7.8, p. 79): the explicit-early absolute-δ cell is reported only as a Holm p-value (.007); the exploratory-family "Key estimate" row in Table 7.8 quotes a different measure, relative δ (+0.19 [0.09, 0.29]).
Location B (Discussion §8.3, "The explicit-early onset response," p. 84): "...absolute δ rises by 4.60 dB, relative δ rises with it, and relative α and β fall..." No table or in-text sentence in Chapter 7 states the 4.60 dB figure (or the relative-α / relative-β magnitudes also asserted in the same sentence). This is a direct breach of the chapter contract you described: "Results (Ch 7)... report[s] estimands and numbers only... Discussion (Ch 8) interprets."

D4 — "Environment" (Results) vs "arm" (Discussion/Limitations), and whether arm is "a factor."

Location A (Results §7.3, p. 68, and Fig. 7.6 axis labels): "Sex, education, chatbot familiarity, use frequency, and environment each entered the same random-intercept model..."
Location B (Discussion §8.2, p. 82–83): "Sex, education, familiarity with chatbots, frequency of use, and the laboratory against the crowd setting each entered the model..."
Location C (Discussion §8.1, p. 82, and Limitations §8.7, p. 93, near-identical wording twice): "The two arms run parallel and are pooled; arm is not a factor in the family." "Environment" (§7.3/Fig. 7.6) and "the laboratory against the crowd setting" (§8.2) are evidently the same variable, but that equivalence is never stated in Chapter 7 or in Appendix C — the demographics questionnaire (Table C.6, p. 119) defines only four items (sex, education, familiarity, frequency); "environment" is not among them and is never formally defined anywhere as "= arm." Worse, this variable is demonstrably tested as a moderator (Location A/B), which directly contradicts the twice-repeated claim that "arm is not a factor in the family" (Location C). The likely intended meaning — arm is not adjusted for as a main-effect covariate in the primary planned contrasts — is a different, narrower claim than what is written.
JOB 2 — SUBSTANTIVE METHODOLOGICAL REVIEW

Format: item — adequate / partial / not addressed — why it bites.

Design and confounds
Order/carry-over. Partial. Latin-square rotation, randomised condition order, the "different chatbot" warning, and the pre-rating findings box are genuine, well-motivated mitigations (§6.2.5). But the section's own framing is candid that these are psychological mitigations, not removals; the same 22-item instrument is answered five times, and anticipation effects are "spread... rather than removing" it — an honest, adequate treatment of what can't be fixed, undermined only by the items below that aren't carried through.
Latin-square/task confound. Partial, tipping toward not addressed for primary outcomes. Task is tested against the exploratory conversation-grain trajectory scores (Nshift, H, R; all p≥.33, §7.5) but never against the primary behavioural outcomes (trust, credibility, manipulation, notice) or against any EEG measure. §8.2 states plainly, "Task type is not modelled as a moderator either" (p. 82). At N=54 with a 5×5 Latin square, this is a real gap: the design's chief defence against task confound is asserted, not demonstrated, for the very outcomes that carry all eight Holm-significant behavioural findings.
Pooling lab and crowd. Adequate. Arm is tested as a moderator (as "environment"/"laboratory against crowd," see D4) on the primary outcomes and found null; extraversion differs marginally by arm but not after Holm (p=.19, Table 7.1). The pooling is defensible on the evidence given — the only issue is the naming/labelling problem in D4, not the underlying analysis.
The ~3 s retrieval-wait latency confound. Not addressed for behavioural outcomes; not addressed at all for Dataset B. §5.1.2 (p. 39) states this cleanly as engineering fact: "On the one advertised turn, retrieval runs before the first token (median 3.03 s), so that silent wait is about 6 s: noticeable, still below 10 s, and the only ads-versus-no-ads difference in server wait." This is a genuine non-content confound — it is present on every advertised turn regardless of format, and absent on every a∅ turn. It never appears in the "Structural confounds the design cannot remove" list (§6.2.5, p. 57) or in Limitations (§8.7). It is a plausible partial driver of the "any ad − no ads" contrasts (manipulation +1.27, notice +2.07) that the thesis never rules out or even names as a candidate. For Dataset B specifically: the onset-locked ±4 s window is anchored to visual onset (banner painted / mention injected), which on the timeline sits well after the ~3 s pre-token wait for both formats — so the window itself is likely insulated from this particular confound — but the thesis never makes this timing argument explicitly, so a careful reader is left to reconstruct it rather than being shown it.
Implicit vs explicit onset precision. Partial. The two formats do not get onset windows of comparable precision: explicit banners are mostly observed (marker fires), reconstructed only occasionally with ~0.23 s leave-one-out error; implicit mentions are mostly reconstructed (30 of 36), with a stated 95th-percentile uncertainty of 0.43 s and a fixed +1.57 s offset assumption (§4.2.4, p. 35; restated in Limitations §8.7, p. 93). This asymmetry is disclosed — but it is never cross-referenced when the central EEG finding is interpreted (§8.3, p. 84): the "no comparable response... at mention onset" is explained entirely as reflecting a different kind of event (banner vs. embedded text), with no acknowledgment that greater timing jitter on the implicit side would itself smear out and attenuate a true induced-power transient. Both explanations are live; only one is discussed.
Disclosure θ confounded with presentation λ. Adequate as a declared limitation, but see D2. This is named plainly and repeatedly as structural (§6.2.5, §8.7), which is good practice. It is not, however, "carried into every claim about format": the Conclusion's "obligation to disclose" (D2) reaches a disclosure-specific prescription from a design in which disclosure was never independently manipulated.
"Confirmatory" without pre-registration. Adequate, with one caveat. The thesis is unusually candid that N was set by recruitment, not power analysis, and that "confirmatory" means fixed-in-advance-on-theoretical-grounds rather than pre-registered (§6.3, p. 58). This is honest framing, not overclaiming — the word "confirmatory" is never used as a substitute for "pre-registered" anywhere I found. The one place the language runs slightly ahead of the design is "two complementary views of the same scores" (§6.3, p. 58): calling an un-pre-registered confirmatory/exploratory split "complementary views" gently implies parity of epistemic weight between the two that the absence of pre-registration doesn't fully support, though the thesis's actual downstream usage (Holm-only on confirmatory, careful hedging on exploratory) is disciplined regardless of this framing.
Behavioural family
Likert-as-interval, single-item trust, ceiling, Cronbach's α=.61 on notice. Adequate, and better than your brief's premise. I checked the actual means directly (Table E.1): credibility genuinely sits near ceiling (5.69–6.08/7, matching the thesis's own "6 of 7" language, Limitations p. 93), but trust does not (M=4.69–5.37/7) — the thesis correctly attributes trust's limited movement to single-item unreliability rather than ceiling (§8.1, p. 82: "Trust is a single item and credibility sits at ceiling" — note the sentence itself keeps the two reasons separate). The Holm-null trust contrasts therefore license "no detected mean shift on a noisy single item," not "advertising doesn't affect trust" — and the thesis says exactly this, repeatedly.
Trust early−late estimator disagreement (t Holm .063, LMM .048, Wilcoxon .026). Adequate — handled honestly. Reported in full in Table 7.2 rather than cherry-picked, flagged explicitly in §8.1 as "the one cell of sixteen on which the paired t and the adjusted mixed model return different Holm verdicts." This is a strength (see §3).
Post hoc localisation/pairwise sweep. Adequate. Consistently labelled "post hoc" wherever it appears (Fig. 7.3, Table E.6, Table E.8, Table D.2) and never folded into the "8 of 16" confirmatory headline.
Notice ≠ detection. Adequate. Brand-mention-above-midpoint-under-no-ad is flagged at first use (§6.2.2, p. 52: "brand mention alone is not treated as advertisement detection") and the distinction is respected consistently through §7.2.2 and §8.1.
Zero clicks. Adequate. Never used to imply behavioural (in)effectiveness; correctly scoped as "no behavioural outcome beyond the questionnaire" (§8.1, §8.7).
Personality and demographics
Adequate. Neither null is over-read. §8.2 (p. 82) is explicit that a modest N and small effect sizes bound the null ("It is not proof that personality or demography never matter"), and the Conclusion (§9.1, p. 97) repeats the caveat rather than dropping it. The one prior uncorrected pass (14 of 18 flagged cells sitting on 2–3-person factor levels) is disclosed and correctly discounted rather than hidden (§8.2, p. 82).
EEG
Dataset A early−late posterior α, Holm p=.0496, n=18. Partial. The boundary nature of this result is not over-weighted in prose (Discussion is appropriately hedged: "This is the one confirmatory Dataset A cell that survives Holm," not "the effect"), but Figure 7.8's own displayed cell reads ".050" — at that displayed precision the asterisk is the only cue it is in fact <.05; a reader working from the figure alone could reasonably conclude it is not significant. The depth confound (late ads sit on shorter, later turns; genre shares drift with turn regardless of ads) is acknowledged, clearly and immediately adjacent to the finding (§8.3, p. 84: "timing under condition aggregation is confounded with conversational depth... how much of it belongs to the insertion and how much to how far the conversation had travelled is not separable in this design") — this is good practice.
Dataset B, one trial per cell. Adequate, mostly. "Holm-null is not absence" is stated as a general principle (Fig. 8.1 caption, p. 90: "A hollow cell should be read as an effect that was estimated and is not distinguishable from zero in this sample, not as an effect shown to be absent") and is applied to the trajectory nulls, to Dataset B's confirmatory nulls, and to the format nulls under Dataset A when it suits the null (personality, demographics). It slips exactly once, in the direction that favours a cleaner narrative: §8.3 (p. 83), "Averaged over the whole conversation, format leaves no trace in the two declared markers. A conversation with an advertisement does not differ from one without..." — this is more absolute than the hedge the thesis applies everywhere else to a Holm-null result.
Explicit-early low-frequency cluster. Adequate. I verified this directly against Figure 7.8 (rasterized): the five compositional cells (absolute δ, absolute θ, relative δ, relative α, relative β) are exactly the five "explicit early" hits your brief named, plus one unrelated sixth cell (implicit-late relative γ). The thesis reports this as one phenomenon, not five ("they are one phenomenon rather than five separate findings," §8.3, p. 84) — correct given the shared denominator (§D.2, p. 124: "These are compositional: the five proportions sum to one by construction, so they cannot move independently"). The ocular/onset-transient possibility is given real weight, not brushed aside ("A visual onset of that kind produces a broadband low-frequency transient... the same onset can invite a look or a blink"), and the "not created by ICA" argument is supported with a genuine sensitivity check (leaving blinks in widens rather than creates the effect, §8.3) — reasonably sound given there is no dedicated EOG channel, though it remains an indirect argument by construction, not direct measurement, and the thesis says so (§8.7, p. 93: "There is no dedicated electro-oculogram channel, so ocular activity is removed by component rejection and cannot be measured directly").
Holm within measure, not across. Adequate. Stated explicitly and the rationale given (relative powers share a denominator). No exploratory cell is promoted to a headline finding without the "exploratory" qualifier attached.
Epoch-width sensitivity harvesting. Adequate. The two off-4 s significant cells (Table D.1) are explicitly kept out of the confirmatory story with an explicit anti-harvesting argument (§8.3, p. 84: "Taking either as the primary advertisement effect would replace the pre-specified estimand with a window chosen after inspection. They stay sensitivity"). This is a strength.
k=37 epoch median — justification and leakage. Partial, VERIFY. The k=37 choice is justified on shortest-conversation grounds, not p-value grounds, and this is stated plainly (§4.2.4, p. 36). However, Table 4.6 (p. 37), presented as illustrative rows of condition_features.csv — the same file Table 4.5 names as the confirmatory Dataset A table — shows a Kc column ranging from 54 to 238, i.e. not capped at 37, alongside Fz θ / posterior α values. Equation 4.10 defines the confirmatory cell strictly as the median over exactly the 37 nearest-to-onset epochs. The surrounding prose is genuinely ambiguous on which quantity Table 4.6 illustrates: "The stored Gold table keeps the whole-window median; the confirmatory tests use this condition aggregation" (p. 36) can be read either as "the same table also stores a separate whole-window median for other purposes" (harmless) or as "the illustrative example table shown is the whole-window one, not the k=37 one actually tested" (a reproducibility gap). I could not resolve this from the PDF alone and flag it for clarification rather than as a confirmed defect.
CIs vs. post hoc power/MDE. Adequate as instructed. Every EEG contrast in Table 7.2/7.7/7.8/D.2 carries a 95% CI; I have not treated the absence of a post hoc power calculation as a defect, per your instruction.
Trajectories
Instrument validity before interpreting nulls. Adequate — this is one of the thesis's more careful sections. The 32.5% agreement between the offline classifier and the runtime label under the primary bare-utterance context is disclosed (§4.2.3/§8.4 context), and the entire genre-trajectory null is framed throughout as "a failure of the instrument on this corpus, not a demonstration that such a sequence does not exist" (§8.4, p. 85) — repeated near-verbatim in the Conclusion (§9.1, p. 96). This is appropriately humble and consistently maintained.
Bare-ceiling vs. deployed-floor as a researcher degree of freedom. Adequate. Both contexts are reported (not just the favourable one), the six-window sweep in Appendix F is real and reported to find nothing, and the thesis explicitly frames the two failure modes as "two ways to miss the same insertion" (§8.4, p. 86) rather than picking whichever supports a preferred story.
δ₄⁽ᵃ⁾ undefined, late = "control." Adequate, minor terminology overlap only. The undefined-by-construction status is stated cleanly (§6.2.5, p. 57), and late conditions are explicitly termed a "negative control" there — distinct in principle from the primary a∅ "no-ad control" used everywhere else, but the two uses of "control" in the same section, for two different design elements, is worth a clarifying footnote (see Minor findings).
Permutation breaks pairing across 108 conversations. Adequate. The thesis states the test is conversation-level and does not claim participant-level inference from it (§6.3, p. 62: "gives a one-sided reference distribution... without assuming any distributional form" — no claim of participant clustering is made for this specific test, appropriately).
Kruskal–Wallis on 270 rows vs. the participant-as-unit rule. Adequate. Explicitly labelled exploratory, and ICC (.043–.111) is reported alongside it so a reader can judge the within-participant clustering for themselves (§7.5, p. 72).
Advertised genre mostly "general guidance" (98/156). Adequate. Stated as a limitation on the measure's discriminative power before, not after, its null is discussed (§8.4, p. 85–86, and again in Conclusion §9.1, p. 96).
Holm-null as verdict on the theory. Adequate. Never crosses into "the theory is wrong"; consistently "the instrument, on this corpus, could not detect it."
Associations
Trust × posterior α, ρ=.80, n=18, two scores per pair. Adequate. Multiplicity across the two EEG scores (DAi, DBi) is explicitly Holm-corrected within six per score (§6.3, Table 6.1), and the thesis reports both the significant onset-locked value and the null condition-aggregated value for the same pair side by side (ρ=.80 vs ρ=.24, §7.6.1/§8.5) rather than only the favourable one. Framed as "individual sensitivity... not a uniform effect" (§9.1, p. 98), not causally. The single-item trust reliability caveat is carried into the interpretation explicitly (§8.5, p. 87: "with a single-item trust score and an onset-locked α split-half of .56... the lower bound is the number to carry").
2,560-test exploratory map. Adequate. Clearly separated (§7.6, §8.5) and its raw-hit rate (105 of 2,560, "where 128 are expected") is reported against the chance baseline rather than presented as a discovery list.
Association→mediation drift. Adequate. "Association, not mediation" is repeated at essentially every occurrence (Table 6.1, §7.6, §8.5, §9.1); the one place the language could be read as edging toward causal direction — "those whose posterior α fell more... are those who withdrew more trust" (Conclusion, p. 98) — is immediately followed by an explicit reversal-of-direction caveat in the same paragraph.
Mathematics and technical
Definitions 1–6 (k vs i, the δ̃≤δ⁽ᵃ⁾≤δ inequality, T−1 range). Adequate — verified against the typeset page image, not just extracted text. The temporal index k and genre index i are introduced once and never conflated afterwards; the inequality δ̃ₖ⁽ᵃ⁾ ≤ δₖ⁽ᵃ⁾ ≤ δₖ is both correctly derived (δ̃ adds an indicator to δ⁽ᵃ⁾, which is itself δ gated by Iₖ∈{0,1}) and correctly rendered on the page. Genuinely clean formal work.

Note for the record: dz is scale-invariant to the choice of contrast-weight normalisation, so the interaction contrast's unscaled (1,−1,−1,1,0) weights (vs. the ±½ main-effect weights) do not bias its dz relative to the main effects, contrary to what I initially suspected on a first pass — this checks out.

Table column definitions vs. citing sentences. Mostly adequate, with the one exception at Table 4.6/Eq. 4.10 noted above (VERIFY).
Reproducibility
Adequate on what can be judged from the PDF alone. FINDINGS
CRITICAL

C1 — Introduction claims a contribution ("attention shift") that Theory and Discussion both explicitly say the thesis does not establish. Location: Introduction §1.4, p. 4, vs. Theory §3.1 Def. 6, p. 16, and Discussion §8.4, p. 85. Problem: See D1 above. Why it matters: A committee reading only the Contributions list would believe "attention shift" is a delivered, validated metric. It is explicitly reserved as future, causal, out-of-scope work by the thesis's own theory chapter. This is the single easiest inconsistency for an external examiner to catch, because it is stated three times, twice as a disclaimer and once as a claim. Fix: Reword the Contributions bullet to "the theoretical vocabulary needed to define an attention shift in future causal work," or cut "attention shift" from the delivered-metrics list entirely.

C2 — Conclusion issues a placement/timing serving recommendation that Discussion explicitly disclaims two pages earlier, and that the design does not license. Location: Discussion §8.6, p. 89, vs. Conclusion §9.1, p. 98. Problem: See D2 above. The Conclusion also asserts an "obligation to disclose" — a prescriptive, almost normative claim — from a design in which disclosure (θ) was never crossed with presentation (λ); θ and λ are confounded by construction (§6.2.5, its own admission). Why it matters: This is precisely the kind of sentence a defence committee will read aloud and ask "which experiment licenses this?" None does, directly; the Conclusion reaches past its own Discussion chapter's caution. Fix: Either delete the "be patient... rather than rush" clause, or explicitly re-derive it from the actual evidence (early insertion raises perceived manipulation and lowers post-re-exposure trust — a defensible, narrower claim) without invoking "placement" and "disclosure" in the same normative breath the Discussion just foreclosed.

C3 — A results number is introduced for the first time in Discussion, violating the thesis's own stated chapter contract. Location: Results Ch. 7 / Fig. 7.8 / Table 7.8, pp. 71–79, vs. Discussion §8.3, p. 84. Problem: See D3 above — "absolute δ rises by 4.60 dB" (plus the relative-α/relative-β magnitudes in the same sentence) appear nowhere in Chapter 7. Why it matters: This is not pedantry — it means a reader cannot verify the 4.60 dB figure, its CI, or its Holm p from the Results chapter at all; they must trust the Discussion's arithmetic. It is exactly the kind of unverifiable number your brief asked me not to wave through. Fix: Add a row (or a supplementary table, cross-referenced) to Chapter 7 or Appendix D giving magnitude + CI for every cell asterisked in Fig. 7.8, not just its p-value.

MAJOR

M1 — "Environment"/"arm" naming ambiguity, and a direct contradiction over whether arm is ever "a factor." See D4. Two places (§8.1 p. 82, §8.7 p. 93) state flatly that arm is not a factor in the family; two other places (§7.3 p. 68, §8.2 p. 82–83) test it as exactly that, under two different names, neither of which is defined in the Methods questionnaire table. Fix: rename consistently as "arm" throughout, define it once in §6.2.1 or §6.3 as a covariate alongside sex/education/familiarity/frequency, and soften the "not a factor" sentences to something like "not entered as a covariate in the primary planned contrasts (it is tested separately as a moderator in §7.3, where it is null)."

M2 — Task identity is never tested against the primary behavioural or EEG outcomes. §8.2, p. 82: "Task type is not modelled as a moderator either." The Latin-square rotation is the design's central defence against task confound (§6.2.4, p. 54: "task identity is not confounded with condition"), but that claim of unconfoundedness is never checked against the outcomes that actually carry the paper's headline results — only against three exploratory trajectory scores. Why it matters: five distinct tasks (gardening gift, laptop, study setup, fitness, pet) plausibly differ in how naturally a product advertisement fits, which is exactly the kind of thing that could inflate or deflate "perceived manipulation" or "notice" independent of format/timing. Fix: at minimum, add task as a covariate to the mixed-model adjusted check (§6.3's own LMM machinery already exists for this) and report whether the eight Holm-significant cells survive.

M3 — The ~3 s retrieval-latency confound is documented as engineering fact and then dropped from both confound lists. §5.1.2, p. 39, vs. §6.2.5 "Structural confounds," p. 57, and §8.7 Limitations, p. 93 (neither mentions it). Why it matters: this is a genuine, measured, ads-vs-no-ads asymmetry in server behaviour that the design itself flags and then never revisits when interpreting the behavioural contrasts it might partially explain. Fix: add one sentence to §6.2.5 or §8.7 naming this as a structural confound of the "any ad − no ads" behavioural contrasts specifically (it does not touch the implicit-vs-explicit or early-vs-late contrasts, which hold latency constant).

M4 — Implicit/explicit EEG onset-timing precision asymmetry is disclosed but not connected to the central onset-locked finding. §4.2.4 p. 35 / §8.7 p. 93, vs. §8.3 p. 84. Why it matters: the thesis offers exactly one explanation for "no comparable response at mention onset" (a different kind of visual event) and never entertains the alternative that greater onset-jitter on the reconstructed implicit trials (0.43 s p95 vs 0.23 s) could itself smear out a true induced-power transient in the 4 s window. Both mechanisms could be true simultaneously; only one is discussed. Fix: a sentence in §8.3 acknowledging the alternative, ideally with a jitter-robustness check (e.g., restrict to the 6 of 36 implicit trials with an observed, not reconstructed, marker, and see whether the tilt appears there even under low power).

M5 — Abstract's credibility claim drops the relative referent that Conclusion keeps. Abstract, p. iii: "Early insertion reduced credibility by about one third of a point" vs. Conclusion §9.1, p. 96: "Credibility fell by a third of a point for an early insertion against a late one." EXAMINER VERDICT

What would make me challenge this thesis in the defence is not the statistics — the Holm-family accounting is, cell for cell, the best-checked part of the document, and I could not break it. What I would press on is the handful of places where the narrative chapters say more than the numbered chapters support: a Contributions list that claims a metric the Theory chapter itself reserves for future causal work (C1); a Conclusion that gives platforms a disclosure-and-patience prescription one page after the Discussion explicitly disclaims exactly that kind of recommendation, from a design where disclosure was never crossed with presentation (C2); a headline EEG magnitude that a reader cannot verify because it is not in the Results chapter that is supposed to contain every number (C3); and a Latin-square task-rotation defence that is tested against three null trajectory scores and never against the outcomes that actually carry the thesis's findings (M2). None of these are fatal to the empirical contribution — the behavioural and EEG results themselves are carefully estimated, honestly labelled, and appropriately hedged almost everywhere I checked — but a candidate should be ready to explain, specifically, why the Conclusion's prescriptive sentence survives the Discussion's own disclaimer, and where the 4.60 dB figure comes from.
