## TLDR conclusions

**Explicit:** notice ↑ · manipulation ↑↑ · recall ↑ · trust / credibility ≈

**Implicit:** notice ↓ · recall ↓ · manipulation ↑

**Early:** credibility ↓ · manipulation ↑ · recall-trust ↓

**Late:** credibility ↑

Explicit-early is the highest manipulation, and the only cell that produces
a trust drop. That is also where the EEG lit up most.

Mean trust ≈ mean posterior α. Neither mean moves. The people whose
posterior α falls more at insertion are the people whose trust falls more.

Ads do not seem to have a steering influence on the conversation beyond
noise.

## Fabricated thesis narrative

**Overall.** Ads have a user-side cost, but it is not as large as we first
thought — good news for companies. The cost lives at insertion, not in the
condition. (Notebook: “a bit too early” on how far to push that.)

Trust does not seem to decrease. Perceived manipulation does (explicit ↑,
early ↑). Credibility decreases when the ad is early. Ads were noticed,
even implicit ones, although product recall is lower for implicit.

Format × timing is not significant. No magic cell.

**Trajectories.** The engineering is not there yet for intent-trajectory
metrics to be used as human-free advertisement-effectiveness scores.
Interesting as a failure report, not as a KPI.

**Combos.** Trust × posterior α covary *during insertion*: those whose
visual cortex responded most to the insertion penalised trust the most.
(Notebook also: “tautological findings.”)

## EEG, two angles

**Condition.** No real advertisement-versus-none state. Posterior α and
relative θ are a bit lower early than late; the two chats are not
equivalent (depth). Writing versus reading still moves Fz θ, so the
pipeline can see engagement when it is there.

**Insertion.** A different story. Against a no-ad baseline, the event
shows — especially explicit-early: slow bands rise, fast bands drop. A
labelled object on screen is a visual onset. A mention inside a streaming
reply is not. Implicit-late is the other lean, not the headline.

## Takeaways

**For AI providers**

1. Average trust does not seem to drop (a priori). That is the trap, not
   the win.
2. Explicit is the pushy, memorable, intrusive ad. It is the one people
   can rate as an ad.
3. Implicit: in the short term they often do not recognise it, and they
   can still feel the steering. They remember the ad less. Suboptimal if
   the point was a brand they can name.
4. Timing changes credibility and pushiness, not notice or recall.
5. No time × format interaction. Do not sell a combo.
6. Trust couples with posterior α at onset. The rest of the EEG battery
   is not discriminative enough for a dashboard.
7. Typing speed and message length lean with the engagement indices at
   implicit − explicit. One exploratory cluster, not a KPI.

**For people**

1. An ad you can recognise is worth more than the “opinion” of the LLM
   or a retrieval ranking with no receipt. Labelled ad > opinion.
2. Ads are probably inevitable for free AI. If the product is free,
   somebody has to pay for it.

No third public takeaway.
