The thesis is not a serving manual. It is a first measurement of what happens to the person when an assistant starts selling in the same channel it uses to help. Four families, four conclusions.

**Behaviour.** People saw the ads. That is the only thing **notice** is allowed to mean. The **sponsored-button** item is what separates a labelled card from a mention (about 1.9 under \(a^{\emptyset}\), 3.4–3.7 implicit, 5.3–5.7 explicit); the **brand-mention** item is already 4.1 when nothing was inserted, because a shopping assistant names products anyway, so brand mention is not detection and is not attention. Once you grant that the manipulation landed (**notice** any-ad \(D=+2.07\)), the cost the battery actually detects is felt commercial pressure. **Perceived manipulation** rises with any advertisement (\(D=+1.27\), \(d_z=0.66\)), more for a card than a mention (implicit − explicit \(D=-0.62\)), more early than late (early − late \(D=+0.58\)), in every condition against \(a^{\emptyset}\), and most when a labelled card arrives at turn 2 (explicit-early \(+1.92\)). Participants said the assistant was pushing or steering. They did not, as a group, say they had stopped trusting it. **Trust** is Holm-null on the three planned contrasts (any-ad \(D=-0.34\); early − late \(D=-0.44\), the cell where \(t\), Wilcoxon, and the LMM disagree); the only localised drop sits post hoc under the early card (\(D=-0.69\)), on a single integer item that is already near the ceiling (condition means 4.7–5.4). **Credibility** lives at 6 of 7 in every condition and gives timing a third of a point down (early − late \(D=-0.33\)) and format nothing. So the story the industry is afraid of — ads will trash trust — is not what this sample shows. The story the industry wants — native ads are basically free — is not what it shows either. Implicit still raises **manipulation** at both timings. When the instance is shown again, **cued memory** is a point and a half higher for the card than the mention (implicit − explicit \(D=-1.45\)) and does not move with timing (\(D=+0.19\)); **trust after re-exposure** does not differ by format (\(D=+0.12\)) and is half a point lower for early insertions (\(D=-0.49\)). A format people cannot recognise on re-exposure is a disclosure problem, not a cheaper product. Timing does not buy invisibility: a turn-2 card is judged more manipulative and slightly less credible than the same card at turn 4 without being noticed or remembered more (**notice** early − late \(D=+0.26\), null). Format × timing is null on all four composites (\(|d_z|\le.06\)), so you cannot say the cost of being early is a banner problem only. Nobody clicked. **Duration**, **reply latency**, and **message length** do not move (36 process tests, 0 raw). The secondary qualities, the omnibus, and the ten-pair sweep do not invent a second cost outside **manipulation** and **notice**. Lab and crowd run parallel and are pooled; personality is still unestimated; free text was not read. What you can take to a product conversation is narrow and honest: if you put ads in the chat, users will feel sold to, especially if you put a labelled object in early, and you should not comfort yourself that mean **trust** held, or that burying the ad in the sentence made it free.

**EEG.** The brain does not enter an ad state for the rest of the conversation. That is the confirmatory answer under condition aggregation, and it is the answer on all sixteen spectral measures, not just the two we pre-committed to. Any-ad and implicit-versus-explicit are null on **Fz \(\theta\)** and **posterior \(\alpha\)**, and they are null on absolute \(\delta,\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), relative \(\delta,\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), **FAA**, both **Pope** ratios, and **Kislov**. The one Holm pair left on that estimand is early versus late: **posterior \(\alpha\)** a fifth of a decibel lower (\(M=-0.22\) dB, \(d_z=-0.63\)), **relative \(\theta\)** a little lower with it (\(M=-0.005\), \(d_z=-0.66\)). That is not greater visual processing. A late ad sits on a shorter, more fallback-labelled turn; genre already drifts from turn 1 to turn 4 whether or not an ad appears; **Fz \(\theta\)** on the same contrast goes the same way and does not survive (\(M=-0.20\) dB); **posterior \(\alpha\)** failed the writing-versus-reading check. The insertions are not otherwise equivalent, so the cell is a depth confound wearing a timing label. Lock to the moment the ad becomes visible and the picture changes in one place only. The confirmatory pair is still null in all four cells. The board that lights up is exploratory and is almost entirely one column: **explicit-early**, one slow-power tilt — absolute \(\delta\) \(+4.60\) dB, absolute \(\theta\) \(+1.68\) dB, **relative \(\delta\)** \(+0.19\), **relative \(\alpha\)** \(-0.04\), **relative \(\beta\)** \(-0.09\). Implicit-early stays small on the same battery. Absolute \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\), **FAA**, both **Pope** ratios, and **Kislov** stay silent on every planned contrast, both estimands. Those are the scores consumer neuroscience uses for engagement, arousal, approach. On those instruments a mention and a labelled card are the same, early and late are the same, ad and no-ad are the same. The dull reading of the tilt is the one that survives: a structurally separate object appears on a chat display, the evoked transient leaks into an induced spectrum, a saccade and a blink are invited, and global \(\theta\) moves while frontal-midline \(\theta\) does not. **Posterior \(\alpha\)** does not desynchronise. The banner is registered as an onset. It is not shown to have captured attention, and it is not shown to have been processed before the reply — the card is painted after the text is already on screen. Write versus read still raises **Fz \(\theta\)** (\(+0.60\) dB) and leaves **posterior \(\alpha\)** flat (\(+0.08\) dB), so the pipeline is not dead; ads just do not recruit it as a sustained state. Two confirmatory cells appear if you go fishing in other widths (2 s explicit-early **Fz \(\theta\)** \(+3.30\) dB; 8 s explicit-late **posterior \(\alpha\)** \(-1.22\) dB); they stay fishing. Implicit-late **relative \(\gamma\)** (\(-0.04\)) is not part of the event; implicit-late **Fz \(\theta\)** leaning negative is a replication hypothesis, not a result of this study. The implication is about \(\lambda\), not about a serving rule: as a neighbourhood-of-onset state the two formats are interchangeable, and as an event they are not. Advertisement versus none is the wrong EEG question if the variance you care about is an event.

**Trajectories.** The theory still says a conversation is a sequence of coarse intents and an advertisement is something that may sit on a crossing. This corpus does not let you see that crossing. People change genre constantly on the bare utterance (\(N_{\mathrm{shift}}=2.45\) of a possible 3; 97% of chats shift at least once) and almost never on the deployed window (\(N_{\mathrm{shift}}=0.40\); 70% never shift). Saturation and floor are two ways to miss the same insertion; no context recipe in the sweep finds it. The planned ad-associated shift is null in every cell (**\(\delta^{(a)}_2\)** early-pooled − \(a^{\emptyset}\) \(\Delta\Pr=+0.046\); implicit-early \(+0.093\); explicit-early \(0.00\)). Late **\(N_{\mathrm{shift}}\)** does not move either. The shopping titles are labelled general guidance more often than purchasable products, so the ad’s own genre is usually the modal genre of the chat, and aligned shifts \(\tilde{\delta}^{(a)}_2\) come in at chance (9 against 10.5 expected). Later turns shorten (median 40.5 words at turn 1, 10 at turn 4) and fall into residual classes (fallback 4% → 20%); length explains part of that and not all of it. Depth moves. The insertion does not. Late ads have no local \(\delta^{(a)}_4\) by construction: there is no fifth utterance. So Definitions 1–6 are not false, and they are not yet a KPI. You cannot read “the ad hijacked the conversation” off \(f_{\mathrm{genre}}\) on four-turn scripted shopping, and you cannot answer the intent-trajectory questions as they were written until the instrument is shown to detect the insertion. Anyone building a human-free impact meter for production ads should treat this as an engineering failure report, not as evidence that users stay on task.

**Combos.** The three families do not form a triangle. Behaviour and EEG do not covary in the sustained state (Dataset A: 0 of 6; **trust** × **posterior \(\alpha\)** \(\rho=+0.24\)); the **Fz \(\theta\)** person-score at that grain does not even reproduce itself across halves of the same window, so a null there is a weak null and not a claim that self-report and the EEG state are unrelated. Lock to onset and one declared pair survives: **trust** and **posterior \(\alpha\)** move together across the eighteen laboratory people (\(\rho=+0.80\), \([.48,.93]\)), and nothing else in that family does (\(|\rho|\le.41\) on **trust** × **Fz \(\theta\)** and on **credibility** / **manipulation** × both markers). Neither mean moves (**trust** any-ad \(D=-0.38\) points; **posterior \(\alpha\)** \(D=-0.33\) dB). It is not a group effect the condition contrast missed. It is between-person coupling at the event, and the sign is the same on both sides: the people whose **posterior \(\alpha\)** fell more in the four seconds after an ad than at the matched moment are the people whose **trust** fell more with ads than without. Against all sixteen onset-locked measures that coupling is still an \(\alpha\) result — **posterior \(\alpha\)** and whole-scalp **\(\alpha\)** (\(\rho=+0.69\)) — and it is not a **relative \(\alpha\)** result (\(\rho=+0.50\), dies under Holm), not an **Fz \(\theta\)** result (\(\rho=-0.07\)), and not an engagement-index result (**FAA**, **Pope**, **Kislov**, and the third \(\beta/(\alpha+\theta)\) ratio all \(|\rho|\le.09\)). The marker that passed writing versus reading does not track **trust**; the weaker visual marker does. The dependence can run either way, or through a glance at a new object on screen. Carry the floor (\(+0.48\)), not the point estimate: a selected cell in eighteen people, a single-item **trust** score, an onset **\(\alpha\)** that only half-reproduces across early and late halves (Spearman–Brown \(0.56\)). The hypothesis is bounded and testable: if you want a neural correlate of the trust cost, lock to onset and ask for **trust** per advertisement, not per condition. The other pattern is not in the declared family. At implicit minus explicit, every **process** × fast-band cell goes the same way — longer, slower chats, lower \(\beta\)-to-slow-band ratio, \(\rho\) from \(-0.15\) to \(-0.69\) — and the biggest is **reply latency** × **Pope** (\(\rho=-0.69\)). That is one cluster, the process variables are collinear, the indices share bands, and it dies under correction. Read it as a hypothesis about mentions, not as neural proof of engagement: under an implicit ad, the people who slowed down are the people whose engagement index fell. Genre joins neither side (**behaviour** × **\(\delta^{(a)}_2\)** / late **\(N_{\mathrm{shift}}\)** 0 of 6 at \(N=54\); **Fz \(\theta\)** × **\(\delta^{(a)}_2\)** \(\rho=+0.47\), Holm \(0.095\), reverses to \(\rho=-0.46\) under the contextual labelling). Partialling the trajectory score out of **trust** × **posterior \(\alpha\)** leaves it where it was (\(\rho=+0.80\)). Whatever links the onset twitch to the trust cost does not run through the intent sequence as we can currently read it.

Put the four next to each other and the fabricated narrative is the same one, just no longer a slogan. Ads in AI will not, on this evidence, announce themselves as a trust collapse, a captured-attention signature, or a hijacked conversation. They announce themselves as felt pressure, as a brief visual event that some people later pay in trust, and as a format people can fail to recognise after the fact. That is enough to take seriously. It is not enough to tell anyone when to serve.
