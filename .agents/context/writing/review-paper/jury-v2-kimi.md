## 2. Findings

### CRITICAL

None. No fatal flaw found. The inferential pipeline (person-level \(D_i\), one-sample \(t\), Holm within declared families, raw Wilcoxon as sensitivity) is correctly specified and correctly executed; every spot-checked table cell reconciles with the condition means; the correction vocabulary is never mixed.

### MAJOR

**M1 — Abstract claims an onset format difference by absence, which the paper itself disclaims.** Location: Abstract, p. 1: "at banner onset an exploratory slow-power tilt appeared and none after the mention". Problem: presence-vs-absence of Holm survival is not a demonstrated difference; §6.2 says so explicitly ("not a demonstrated difference between the formats", smallest direct-comparison Holm \(p=.08\)), and the Conclusion carries the qualifier while the Abstract drops it. Why it matters here: the Abstract is the surface a PC member quotes; as written it reports a format effect at onset that the design did not show, on top of the windowing asymmetry in M4. Change: import the Conclusion's qualifier into the Abstract ("…a tilt at banner onset, with no demonstrated difference from the mention").

**M2 — §D.2 mis-counts the exploratory Holm cells, contradicting Table 19 and §5.4.** Location: p. 39, quoted in D1. Problem: "six of the seven belong to the explicit-early onset contrast" is wrong; five do. Why it matters: the paper's strongest suit is internal consistency, and this is a flat numeric contradiction between an appendix paragraph, its own table, and the Results — exactly the kind of slip that makes a reviewer re-audit everything else. Change: "five of the seven belong to the explicit-early onset contrast and are read as one slow-power tilt; the sixth onset-locked cell is implicit-late relative \(\gamma\)."

**M3 — The only confirmatory EEG survival is a boundary cell that is depth-confounded by construction and was never width-checked, yet it carries RQ6, a Discussion paragraph, and an Abstract sentence.** Locations: §5.4 (p. 12): "the only Holm cell is early minus late on posterior \(\alpha\) (\(-0.22\) dB, \([-0.39, -0.04]\), \(d_z=-0.63\), Holm \(p=.0496\); raw Wilcoxon \(p=.021\))"; §D.1 (p. 38): "The width grid was **not** run on the Dataset A onset-centred aggregation, so its early-minus-late posterior \(\alpha\) cell is reported at the pre-specified 4 s **only**"; §6.2 (p. 15): "timing under condition aggregation is confounded with conversational depth, and how much of the cell belongs to the insertion is not separable in this design"; Table 5 (p. 16): RQ6 "Partly … early − late posterior \(\alpha\) −0.22 dB, Holm \(p=.0496\)". Problem: three facts compound — (a) \(p=.0496\) at \(n=18\) is a boundary cell; (b) because Dataset A is the 37 epochs *nearest the onset*, early ≈ epochs around turn 2 and late ≈ epochs around turn 4, so the cell is a conversational-position contrast as much as an insertion-timing one; (c) it is the one confirmatory cell in the paper that has no sensitivity re-estimation at any other epoch width, while all eight Dataset B cells were re-run at four widths. The paper declares (b) and (c) — but then Table 5 still answers RQ6 "Partly" on this cell alone, and the Abstract reports it with no confound flag. Change: (i) report the cheap, decisive control that the design already contains — the early-vs-late Dataset A contrast *under \(a^\emptyset\)* (the matched turn-2/turn-4 replies exist; §E.2 names them), which would quantify the pure depth effect; (ii) run the width grid on the Dataset A cell or state why it cannot be run; (iii) name the confound inside Table 5's RQ6 evidence cell, not only in §6.2.

**M4 — Dataset B is not like-for-like across formats at the window level, and the paper never says so.** Location: §E.3 (p. 47): "onset is reconstructed as the assistant reply plus 0.49 s for explicit banners, and as the injection event plus 1.57 s for implicit mentions, **the latter falling before the reply has finished streaming**"; §4.2/§6.5 on the ~3 s retrieval wait on advertised turns. Problem: by the paper's own statement the reconstructed mention onset sits *inside* the streaming reply, so the mention's pre-onset window \([t-4, t)\) contains reply text, while the banner's pre-window is dominated by the silent retrieval wait (a wait that is itself ~3 s longer on advertised turns, §6.5). The post−pre contrast therefore subtracts different baselines per format, which mechanically attenuates mention cells relative to banner cells. The paper discusses onset *error magnitude* (0.43 s vs 0.23 s) and refuses the direct format comparison — but never addresses what the two pre-windows *contain*. Why it matters: it is a second, mechanical reason the "tilt at banner, none at mention" pattern cannot be read as format (M1), and it limits even the mention-versus-matched-control cells, since the matched \(a^\emptyset\) pre-window is the wait. Change: state the pre-window content per format; report the distribution of the mention's streaming offset; consider a reply-start-locked control for the implicit cells.

### MINOR

The wording is honest and the four-tier label system is well defined, but the label's evidentiary weight is unverifiable, and Table 18 (p. 38) shows two Holm-surviving cells at 2 s and 8 s that would have been "findings" under a different fixed choice. - **m9 — ICA direction sits awkwardly with the blink account.** §6.2 (p. 15): "Without ICA the \(\delta\) cell keeps its sign but not its significance (+3.04 dB, Holm \(p=.29\)…) so the cleaning did not invent the direction, and the cell's dependence on ocular correction is one more reason to read it as an onset transient"; §D.1 (p. 38): the cell "*shrinks* from +4.60 dB (Holm \(p=.007\)) to +3.04 dB" without ICA — i.e., ocular-component removal *enlarged* the \(\delta\) rise. With realised cells from 5 to 17 (Table 15), a task-random-effect sensitivity on the four primary outcomes would cheaply close this.

## 3. Top 5 vulnerabilities

1. The neurophysiological headline is a single boundary cell (early−late posterior \(\alpha\), Holm \(p=.0496\), \(n=18\)) that is confounded with conversational depth by the onset-centred window construction, was never re-estimated at any other epoch width, and sits beside an entirely null onset-locked confirmatory family.
2. The Abstract asserts by absence a format difference at onset ("a slow-power tilt appeared and none after the mention") that the paper's own Discussion disclaims (direct comparison Holm \(p=.08\)), and the reconstructed mention onset falls mid-stream, so the asymmetry may be manufactured by the windowing rather than the format.
3. "Confirmatory" is asserted without pre-registration while every processing degree of freedom (4 s width, 37-epoch count, ICA thresholds, 1,050 μV rejection) is self-reported as fixed a priori — and Table 18's two off-width Holm-surviving cells show how easily the confirmatory board could have told a different story.
4. The behavioural headline (+1.27 felt manipulation for any advertisement) is, by the authors' own admission, the advertisement plus its ~3 s retrieval delay, and the construct the title promises — trust — is null on every planned contrast, measured with a single item, while credibility sits at 6 of 7.
5. The paper's most quotable number, \(\rho=.80\), rests on 18 participants, a single-item trust difference whose mean is null (\(-0.38\), \(p=.38\)), and an onset-locked score with split-half reliability .56 — a strong prior for a replication that shrinks.

## 4. Three strongest aspects

1. **The analysis contract.** Table 3 plus §4.5 fix the inferential unit, the three planned weight vectors, the estimators, and the correction families once, and the discipline is total: Holm \(p\) is always the adjusted paired \(t\), Wilcoxon is always raw, the interaction sits outside the family with a raw \(p\), and every spot-checked cell in Tables 4, 7, 8, 11, 20, 21, 22 reconciles exactly with the condition means.
2. **The within-conversation positive control.** Writing minus reading raises Fz \(\theta\) by +0.60 dB (Holm \(p=.007\)) on the same pipeline before any advertisement contrast is read, which converts the format nulls from "nothing works" into "nulls on a marker this cohort can move" — more than most \(n=18\) EEG studies can say.
3. **Self-limiting interpretation.** The depth confound, the TTFT confound, compositional relative powers, "98 uncorrected tests at \(\alpha=.05\) would produce about five false positives", the trust early−late estimator straddle (\(t\) .063 / LMM .048 / Wilcoxon .026), and the quarantined off-width cells are all declared before a reviewer can weaponise them; post hoc is labelled post hoc even in the Abstract.

## 5. Scores (1–10)

- Research question / contribution: **8** — a real gap (user-side cost, format × timing crossed in the same people, plus EEG), cleanly posed; the behavioural half largely replicates known detection/manipulation patterns at higher rigour.
- Design: **7** — within-participant crossing with Latin-square task rotation and a warm-up; deductions for the TTFT confound on any-ad cells, one exposure per cell, four-turn ecological ceiling, and arm pooling.
- Statistical validity: **8** — declared families, correct Holm/Wilcoxon discipline, sensitivity lattice (bootstrap, LMM, item-level, width, no-ICA); deductions for the unverifiable confirmatory label and the boundary cell's weight.
- Technical correctness: **8** — equations, weights, and arithmetic verified; deductions for the D.2 count error and the unaddressed window-content asymmetry.
- Results / interpretation discipline: **9** — Results report, Discussion interprets, post hoc is labelled everywhere including the Abstract; one Abstract sentence (M1) breaks the rule.
- Internal consistency: **7** — very high overall, but D1, D2, D4, D5 are real cross-section contradictions.
- Reproducibility: **8** — filters, ICA thresholds, epoch rules, Welch settings, bands, electrodes, weights, and families are all on the page and the data/code are released; the 22 items are not reprinted and two are unmapped.
- Writing / short-form craft: **9** — dense, controlled, figure-first prose; a few over-long sentences and register slips.
- **Overall: 7** — weak accept as it stands.

## 6. Reviewer verdict

What would make me argue weak reject in the PC meeting: if the PC weighs the EEG contribution as one depth-confounded boundary cell at \(n=18\) plus nulls, and the behavioural contribution as a careful replication of known detection/manipulation findings with trust flat and zero clicks, then the novel residue is the taxonomy, the two-estimand EEG methodology, and the dataset — solid but incremental, and the Abstract currently oversells the onset result (M1) that the methodology section itself refuses to claim. What holds me at weak accept: the analysis contract is the most disciplined I have reviewed this cycle, the positive control gives the nulls teeth, every number I audited reconciles, and the dataset plus the onset-locked methodology are genuine community assets. The path to keeping my vote is cheap: fix the Abstract sentence, correct the D.2 count, and add the \(a^\emptyset\) early-vs-late depth control (the windows already exist) or the Dataset A width grid; if neither can be produced, RQ6's "Partly" should come out of Table 5.
